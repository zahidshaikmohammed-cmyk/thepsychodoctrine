# WEAPON 172 — THE PRODUCT OBLITERATION

Explain the problem the product is designed to address—not just its features.
Be specific about what it can do and what it cannot.
Do not promise to erase every personal difficulty or guarantee a transformation.
Compare the product with alternatives and the option of doing nothing.
A clear outcome is more persuasive than dramatic destruction language.
Remove confusion, not the buyer’s judgment.
Let the value stand on its merits.
That is product clarity.
