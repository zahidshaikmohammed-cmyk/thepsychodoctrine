# Rebuttals — Weapons 10–40

## 10 — The Client Fracture
**Client:** “I'll wait. I'll get into it when the timing feels right.”

I know people who waited… and still regret it. [pause] And I know people who rushed and regret that more. So let's not pretend waiting is the enemy.

The real question is what you're waiting *for*. If it's information — what this is, what it costs you, what happens when it goes wrong — that's not waiting, that's homework, and I'll help you do it tonight. If it's a feeling, a moment when the fear finally goes quiet — that moment doesn't come. Not for anyone. The fear doesn't leave. It just gets outvoted by a plan.

So here's what regret actually looks like. It isn't missing one move. It's looking back and realising you never decided anything at all — you just let time decide for you.

You can still say no. A clear no is a decision, and I respect it. What I won't do is help you drift.

So tell me — what would have to be true for the timing to feel right?

## 11 — The Ego Thread
**Client:** “I've been trading for twelve years. I don't need anyone holding my hand.”

You look like someone who never misses a move. [pause] So I won't insult you by explaining what a stop-loss is.

But let me ask you something only a serious trader would answer honestly. In twelve years, where has your edge actually come from? Not the wins you remember — the pattern underneath them. Because experience cuts both ways. It sharpens instinct. It also hardens habits. Every trader I respect has one blind spot that their own record keeps hiding from them, and the better they are, the better it hides.

I'm not here to hold your hand. I'm here to put a second set of eyes on your process — the kind you can test, argue with and throw out if it doesn't earn its place.

If I can't show you one thing in your own trades you hadn't already seen, you've lost twenty minutes and proved me wrong. If I can — that's the edge you didn't know you were leaving on the table.

Want to put your last ten trades on the table?

## 12 — The Frame of Control
**Client:** “Just tell me the returns. That's all I care about.”

Here's what matters. [pause] And it isn't the number you just asked for.

Anyone who quotes you returns on a first call is selling you a story. I don't know what the market will do, and neither does anyone else on this planet. If I gave you a number, you'd hold me to it — and you'd be right to — and I'd have started this relationship with a lie.

So let me reset the order. First: what you're trying to make this money do, and by when. Second: how much of it you can watch go down without making a panic decision. Third: how this actually works — the mechanics, the costs, the risks. Then we talk about what's realistic, with ranges, not promises, and the bad scenarios laid next to the good ones.

That's not me dodging your question. That's me refusing to answer it badly.

I've been here before. I'll be here after. So — what's this money for?

## 13 — The Emotional Drill
**Client:** “I'm not emotional about money. I just look at the numbers.”

Good. Then let's look at a number nobody shows you. [pause]

Ever felt you knew it… but still didn't act? You saw the setup. You did the work. The logic was clean. And you sat on your hands — and watched it run without you.

That's not a numbers problem. Your numbers were right. Something else stepped in between the analysis and the action. Call it caution, call it doubt, call it a bad week — but it's the most expensive line on any trader's statement, and it never shows up as a figure.

I'm not saying you're emotional. I'm saying you're human, and humans freeze in exactly the moments the numbers say move — or move in exactly the moments they say wait.

So the question isn't whether you can read the numbers. You can. The question is whether you've got a process that holds when you don't trust yourself.

Tell me about the last one you saw coming — and didn't take.

## 14 — The Dominance Pressure
**Client:** “I'm speaking to three other firms. I'll get back to you.”

Good. You should. [pause] I won't chase. I only offer once.

Not because the offer disappears — it doesn't. Because chasing you would mean I'm more invested in this decision than you are, and that's the wrong way round.

So let me make the comparison easy, because most people compare the wrong things. Don't compare promises — everyone has those. Compare four things. Who regulates them, and can you verify it in two minutes. What it costs you, all-in, written down. What happens on the worst day — how fast you can get out and who picks up the phone. And whether the person on the other end ever told you something you didn't want to hear.

Put us all side by side on those four. If someone beats us, go with them. I'll mean it when I say well chosen.

I'll send you that checklist now. When do you expect to decide — this week, or next?

## 15 — The Trust Ripper
**Client:** “I don't trust brokers. You're all the same.”

I know you've heard bullshit before. [pause] So let's not play expert — let's play honest.

You're right to be suspicious. This industry has earned it. People were promised returns that never came, sold products they didn't understand, and left alone the moment the money cleared. If that happened to you, or someone close to you, distrust isn't a flaw. It's a scar that's doing its job.

So I'm not asking you to trust me. Trust is the end of this process, not the start.

Here's what you can verify without taking my word for anything. [Firm]'s registration with [regulator] — number [registration no.], checkable online in two minutes. Where client money is held, and whether it's kept separate from ours. Every fee, in writing, before you commit a rupee. And my name, so you know exactly who to hold responsible.

If any of that doesn't check out, hang up and never take my call again.

Want the details now, so you can check them while we talk?

## 18 — The Energy Flip
**Client:** “I'm not interested.”

That's why I called. [pause]

Not to change your mind — I don't know enough about you to do that yet. But "not interested" usually means one of three things. You've heard pitches like this before and they wasted your time. You're happy with what you've got. Or the timing's genuinely wrong.

If it's the first, fair — give me thirty seconds to prove I'm different, and if I'm not, you've lost thirty seconds. If it's the second, I'd honestly like to know what's working for you; it tells me whether I can add anything at all. If it's the third, tell me when, and I'll call then — not before.

And if what's really underneath it is "I don't trust brokers" — exactly. Let me earn it. Not with promises. With things you can check.

Which one is it?

## 19 — The Scriptless Slap
**Client:** “I keep going back and forth. I don't know what's holding me back.”

Look, I don't know if you're scared or smart… [pause] …but this isn't about investing. It's about changing direction.

Because here's what I hear. You're not short of information. You've read, you've watched, you've asked around. And you're still exactly where you were six months ago. That tells me the question isn't "is this good?" It's "am I the kind of person who does this?"

Scared and smart can look identical from the outside. Smart says: I'm not doing this until I understand the downside. Scared says: I'm not doing this, full stop, and I'll find a new reason every time.

If you're smart, let's go through the downside right now — properly, no sugar. If you're scared, that's fine too, but then let's say it out loud, because a fear you name is a fear you can measure.

So which one is talking right now?

## 20 — The Authority Injection
**Client:** “Why should I listen to you? You're just a salesman.”

Here's what's happening — whether you join or not. [pause]

The market opens tomorrow. Prices move. People with a plan act on it; people without one react to it. That doesn't change because of this call. The only thing this call can change is which of those two you are.

And yes, I sell. I won't hide it. Here's what I am, precisely: [role], with [Firm], registered under [registration/licence]. Here's what I'm not: I'm not a fortune-teller and I don't predict prices. Anyone who claims they can is the person you shouldn't listen to.

What I can do is show you how this works, what it costs, what can go wrong, and how to protect yourself when it does. Judge me on whether that's useful — not on my title.

Fifteen minutes. If I've said nothing you couldn't have found yourself, you'll know exactly what I am. Fair test?

## 21 — The Client Mirror
**Client:** “I've done my research. I know what I'm looking at.”

Sounds like you've done your research. [pause]

So why aren't you inside already?

I'm asking seriously, not as a trick. When someone has done the work and still hasn't moved, there's always a gap between the research and the action — and that gap is where the real answer lives. Sometimes it's money: the capital isn't ready. Sometimes it's risk: the research showed the upside clearly and the downside not clearly enough. Sometimes it's trust: you believe the idea, not the people offering it. And sometimes the research quietly told you no, and you haven't admitted it yet.

Every one of those is a valid reason. But only one of them is yours, and until we know which, we're both guessing.

So let me mirror it back: you understand this, you've looked hard, and something is still stopping you.

What is it?

## 22 — The Time Razor
**Client:** “There's no rush. I'll look at it properly later.”

The chart doesn't wait, and neither do I. [pause] But let me be precise about what that means, because I'm not going to fake urgency on you.

Nothing I'm offering expires tonight. If someone tells you a deal vanishes at midnight, walk away — that's a pressure tactic, not an opportunity.

What does move is the market itself. This candle expires with or without your action — that's not a threat, it's just how time works. Prices you see today won't be there next month, for better or worse. You might be glad you waited. You might not. Nobody knows, and anyone who says otherwise is lying.

So "later" isn't wrong. It just has to mean something. Later with a date and a decision attached is a plan. Later with nothing attached is a habit.

Pick the day you'll decide — yes or no — and I'll respect either answer. What day is it?

## 24 — The Pressure Zone
**Client:** “Maybe. Let me see.”

I don't want a maybe — I want a move. [pause] And "no" counts as a move.

Here's why. A maybe costs both of us. You carry it around — it sits in the back of your head every time the market moves. I carry it in my diary. Nobody wins. A clear no frees you completely. A clear yes gives you something to act on. A maybe just keeps the door half open and lets the draught in.

So let's turn it into something real. What would turn this into a yes? Name it — a number, a question, a person you need to speak to. And what would make it a firm no? Name that too.

If the no is stronger, say it. I'll thank you, close the file, and you'll never hear from me again.

If the yes is stronger, we take the next small step.

So, which is heavier right now?

## 25 — The Frequency Thread
**Client:** “I'm scared of making the wrong decision.”

You know this already. You're just scared to confirm it. [pause]

That's not a weakness. It's the most honest thing anyone's said to me today.

Here's how fear works with money. It doesn't care about the size of the decision; it cares about whether you can undo it. So let's make this decision undoable. Not all in. Not life savings. An amount you've already decided you can afford to lose — not want to, *can* — with a written plan for what happens if it goes wrong, and when you'll stop.

Now ask yourself: what does wrong actually look like? Put a number on it. If that number is survivable — if you'd be annoyed, not damaged — then the fear is louder than the risk, and you can act. If it isn't survivable, the fear is right, and we make the amount smaller or we don't do it at all.

So what's the number that would hurt but not break you?

## 26 — The Shame Torque
**Client:** “I always miss these. I saw it coming last time and did nothing.”

What will it feel like when this runs without you again? [pause] You already know — you just described it.

But let's be careful with that feeling. Regret is a terrible strategist. It makes you chase the move you missed instead of preparing for the next one. People who trade out of regret buy late, buy big, and buy at exactly the wrong price.

So don't act to make the regret go away. Act because you've got a process that would have caught the last one — and will catch the next one.

Are you okay watching others act while you overthink? Probably not. But the answer isn't to stop thinking. It's to think in advance — decide your rules before the moment comes, so when it does, you're executing, not agonising.

Let's write one rule together — the one that would have put you in last time. What would it be?

## 27 — The 4-Word Collapse
**Client:** “Just give me the short version.”

It's already too late. [pause] For the version of this decision you could make without thinking. You're thinking now. Good.

You know this moment. You've been here before — interested, unsure, busy. And you know how it usually ends: the call finishes, life takes over, and nothing changes.

So here's the short version, in four parts. What it is: [one-line product description]. What it can do: [the realistic benefit]. What it can cost you: real money, if it goes wrong — and I'll show you exactly how much. What happens next: one small step, nothing irreversible.

Or wait. Miss again. That's an option too — I'm not taking it off the table. But if you choose it, choose it on purpose.

Four words, then. Yes, no, or later?

## 28 — The Exit Seal
**Client:** “Alright. Let's do it.”

Good. I'm locking this in for you now. [pause] But before I do, thirty seconds — because a decision this size deserves to be sealed properly, not rushed.

Here's exactly what you're agreeing to: [product], starting with [amount], with the risk we discussed — you can lose some or all of what you put in. Here's what happens next: I send you the paperwork and the full terms in writing. You read them. Then you sign, not before.

Once I send this, it's sealed — but you're never trapped. Here's how you get out if you change your mind: [cancellation / withdrawal process].

Any question you've been holding back to be polite — now's the time. Not after the money moves.

No? Then the paperwork is on its way. Check your inbox in five minutes, and let's book fifteen minutes on [day] to walk through your first step together.

## 30 — The Yes Spark
**Client:** “Okay. Yes. I'm in.”

Done. [pause]

No fireworks. You don't need them, and I won't insult this decision with hype. You didn't say yes because I talked you into it. You said yes because you went through it, looked at the downside, and decided you could live with it. That's the only kind of yes worth having.

So let's make sure it stays that way. Over the next forty-eight hours you might wake up at 3 a.m. wondering if you made a mistake. That's normal. It's called buyer's nerves, and everyone gets it. When it happens, look at the plan we wrote — the amount, the limit, the exit. If it still makes sense in daylight, carry on. If it doesn't, call me, and we'll talk it through, no pressure, no guilt.

Welcome. You're not a customer to me. You're the reason I have to be right.

First step: [next action]. Shall we do it now, or first thing tomorrow?

## 31 — The Pressure of Decision
**Client:** “I can't decide. I keep going round in circles.”

This is the decision. There's nothing else now. [pause] Not because you're out of time — because you're out of new information.

Going round in circles usually means one thing: you're re-running the same questions, hoping they'll give a different answer. They won't. You've heard what this is, what it costs, what can go wrong. What's left isn't analysis. It's weighing.

So let's weigh it properly. Write three columns. What you gain if this works. What you lose if it doesn't. What happens if you do nothing at all — because that's a choice with consequences too. Score each one honestly.

If the loss column outweighs the gain, the answer is no, and that's a strong, clean decision. If the gain outweighs it and the loss is survivable, the answer is yes. If they're level, start smaller.

Pressure isn't me pushing you. It's the weight of the choice itself. So which column is heaviest?

## 32 — The Silent Command
**Client:** [long silence after the full explanation]

Do you feel the truth in the silence? [pause]

I'm not going to fill it. You're thinking, and that's more important than anything I could say right now.

When you're ready, tell me what's going on in that pause. Because silence after a proposal is usually one of two things. Either you're seeing it click into place — the fit, the logic — and you're getting used to the idea. Or something doesn't sit right, and you're working out whether to say it.

If it's the second one, say it. The thing you're not saying is the most important thing in this call. I'd rather hear "this part worries me" than hear "sounds great" and never hear from you again.

And if it's the first — sit with it. There's no rush.

What's on your mind?

## 33 — The Invisible Force
**Client:** “I don't really see what's different about this.”

You don't see it, but you feel it. [pause] And you're right not to trust a feeling. So let me make the invisible visible.

On the surface, most offers look the same — same charts, same apps, same promises. The difference never sits on the brochure. It sits in the things you only see when something goes wrong. How fast you can exit on a violent day. Whether someone answers when you call. Whether the risk rules are explained before you trade, or after you've lost. Whether the person advising you gets paid more for steering you somewhere particular.

So here's the test. Ask me the hardest question you'd ask on your worst day in the market — the one where everything's falling and you need help. I'll answer it now. Then ask whoever you're with now the same question.

The difference isn't what you see. It's what holds when nothing else does.

What's that question?

## 34 — The Art of Dominance
**Client:** “Go on then. Convince me.”

I'm not here to convince you. I'm here to lead you. [pause]

And I mean that literally. Convincing is what people do when they're not sure of their own case. They talk louder, faster, they stack promises until you say yes just to make it stop. You've had those calls. You hung up on most of them — and you were right to.

I'd rather do it differently. You tell me three things. What you want this money to do. How much of it you can afford to see go down without losing sleep. And what's stopped you acting before. That's it.

Then I walk you through exactly how this would work against those three answers. The upside, the downside, and where it doesn't fit you — because there will be places it doesn't. If what I show you doesn't stand on its own, no amount of convincing should move you, and I won't try.

You're not being sold. You're being shown the road. Whether you walk it is your call, not mine.

So — first question. What do you actually want this money to do?

## 35 — The Final Decision Impact
**Client:** “I haven't made up my mind yet.”

You've already chosen. You just haven't realized it. [pause] Let me show you what I mean — and if I'm wrong, tell me.

You've spent forty minutes on this call. You've asked about risk, about how to get out, about what happens on bad days. People who've decided no don't ask how to get out. They ask how to end the call. Your questions are the questions of someone working out how to do this safely.

But I could be reading that wrong, so let's check it instead of assuming. If you said yes right now, what would worry you most tomorrow morning? And if you said no, what would you feel — relief, or that itch you've had for months?

Whichever answer comes faster is your real decision. Your head will argue with it, and that's fine — that's what heads are for. But it's already there.

Which one came first?

## 36 — The Systematic Pressure Point
**Client:** “I'll wait for a better price.”

Every second you wait, the price changes. [pause] Notice I didn't say increases. Nobody knows which way it goes. Anyone who tells you the price only goes up is selling you a fantasy.

So waiting for a better price is really a bet — a bet that you can time the market better than the market. Professionals with screens, algorithms and research teams lose that bet half the time. That's not an insult; it's maths.

Here's the pressure point that actually matters. If the idea is good, a slightly worse price won't kill it. If the idea only works at a perfect price, it isn't a good idea — it's a lottery ticket.

So there are two clean options. Decide the price you'd be happy with, set it, and let the order wait for you instead of you waiting for the market. Or split the entry across a few weeks and stop trying to be perfect.

Which one fits how you think?

## 37 — The Closing Force
**Client:** “I'm interested. Let's see how it goes.”

This isn't a decision anymore. This is a commitment. [pause] And that's exactly why "let's see how it goes" isn't enough.

"Let's see" is how people start gym memberships in January. Interest is cheap — it costs nothing to feel. Commitment is different. It means you've decided what you're putting in, what you'll do when it goes against you, and what success looks like before you begin.

Without that, you're not investing. You're spectating with money.

So if you're in, let's be in properly. Three things, agreed now. The amount — and it must be money you can afford to lose. The rule that gets you out if it goes wrong. The date we review it together, whatever happens.

If you can commit to those three, we start. If you can't yet, that's an honest answer, and it means you're not ready — no shame in that.

So — amount, exit, review date. Can you name them?

## 38 — The Pressure of the Unseen
**Client:** “I don't fully understand how this works.”

You can't see it, but you feel it. [pause] And that feeling — the unease — is your judgment working properly. Never put money into something you can't explain to a friend.

So let me take it apart for you, piece by piece. [One-sentence explanation of how the product works.] Here's where the money goes. Here's how it can grow. Here's how it can shrink — and how fast. Here's what you pay, and when. Here's how you get out.

That's the whole machine. Nothing hidden underneath it.

Now, the important part: I want you to explain it back to me in your own words. Not to test you — to test me. If you can't explain it, I've explained it badly, and we go again until you can.

The unseen is only frightening until someone turns the light on. Go ahead — tell me how it works.

## 39 — The Final Close
**Client:** “What if it doesn't work out?”

Once you make this decision, there's no looking back. [pause] But that doesn't mean there's no way out. Those are different things, and the difference matters.

No looking back means you stop relitigating it every morning. You made the call with the best information you had, and you let the plan do its job.

A way out is something we build before you start. Here it is. First: you decide your maximum loss — the number at which you stop, no debate, no hope. Second: the money you put in is money you could lose without changing how you live. Third: you know exactly how to withdraw — [withdrawal process] — and how long it takes.

So if it doesn't work out, here's what happens: you hit your limit, you stop, you're annoyed, and your life carries on. That's not failure. That's a plan working.

So what's your number — the point where you'd stop?

## 40 — The Art of Persistence
**Client:** “Look, stop calling me.”

You've already made the choice; I'm here to guide you. [pause] And the choice you've made is no. So let me guide you out of my list, properly.

I'll remove your number today, and you won't hear from me again. That's a promise, and I keep those.

Before I go — one sentence, and then I'm gone. If you ever do decide to look at this, whether with me or anyone else, check three things first: who regulates them, what it costs in writing, and what happens on the worst day. That will protect you from people who don't take no as well as I do.

Persistence isn't calling someone who's asked you to stop. That's just noise. Persistence is being the person they remember was straight with them when they finally are ready.

Your number comes off the list today. Take care of yourself.
