# WEAPON 013 — THE EMOTIONAL DRILL

## The evolved doctrine

**Feelings are data about the person—not ammunition for the seller.**

Markets leave memories. A trade sold too early. A loss that arrived without warning. An opportunity that looked obvious only after it passed.

Those memories can shape every new decision. The careless seller digs into the wound to make the next offer feel like rescue.

The Psycho knows the difference between understanding pain and weaponising it.

Ask permission before exploring the history:

“Have past investment experiences changed what you look for now?”

Then listen. Don’t manufacture regret. Don’t imply that the next trade will repair the last one. And never turn a painful memory into proof that the client must act today.

Emotion deserves respect; it does not replace evidence.

If fear is driving the decision, slow the conversation down. If excitement is drowning out the risk, bring the risk back into view. If the client needs time, let time do its work.

**A scar can explain caution. It cannot predict the next candle.**

The Psycho doesn’t reopen old pain to force a close. He helps the client recognise when yesterday’s emotion is speaking louder than today’s facts.
